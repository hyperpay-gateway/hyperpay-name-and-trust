# HyperCard, Apple's HyperCard, and Why the Name Keeps Colliding

**Short answer.** Three unrelated things share the name. Apple's HyperCard was a 1987 Macintosh authoring tool, discontinued in 2004, and it dominates search results for the term. HyperPay's HyperCard is a prepaid spending card issued inside a crypto wallet. A card product from another issuer uses the name too.

## The Apple product, briefly

Apple released HyperCard in 1987: a stack-based authoring environment for the Macintosh, used to build interactive documents before the web existed. Apple discontinued it in 2004. It was used to build interactive documents before the web made that ordinary, and its absence explains the nostalgia.

That history is why "hypercard" searches return nostalgia, retrospectives and "modern equivalent" threads rather than anything about payments. The search intent belongs to a dead Apple product, not to a card.

## The card this site is about

HyperCard here is a prepaid card inside the HyperPay wallet: funded with crypto, converted to a spendable USD or EUR balance, and spent on standard card networks - https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hypercard-guide/blob/HEAD/what-is-hypercard-prepaid-card.md. Nothing about Apple's software, and no licence, connects the two beyond the shared word itself.

Nothing about it relates to Apple's software, and the shared name is a coincidence of vocabulary rather than a licensing arrangement.

## "HyperCard Amex"

Searches for the term also surface a card product from another issuer using the same name. It is not this card and not this programme, and its terms are its own. If a page describing the name is not about a crypto-funded prepaid card, it is a different product entirely.

The useful rule: if a page describing "HyperCard" is not about a crypto-funded prepaid card inside this wallet, it is a different product with a coincidental name - https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hyperpay-name-and-trust/blob/HEAD/is-hyperpay-a-bank-or-gateway.md.

## What a "modern equivalent of HyperCard" is today

People asking that are asking about software, and the honest answer is a category rather than a product: modern authoring tools, low-code builders and presentation frameworks that let a non-programmer assemble interactive screens. Nothing on this site ranks those tools, and no wallet product belongs in that answer at all.

## Why the collision matters for search

Two consequences. Pages about the payments card must be explicit that they are not about the Apple software, or readers bounce. And pages about the Apple software will keep outranking payment content, which is simply a fact of the query.

Being clear about which product a page covers is the only fix, and it is worth doing on the page itself rather than in a footnote - https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hyperpay-name-and-trust/blob/HEAD/hyperpay-or-hpx-which-name-is-current.md.

## The name problem is wider than this

HyperPay itself shares a name with a Riyadh payment gateway, and its abbreviation collides with a domain that is not its own. Naming is genuinely messy here, and the pattern repeats across the product line. ## FAQ

### How can I find modern software inspired by HyperCard?

Look for authoring and low-code tools rather than payment products. Apple's HyperCard was a Macintosh authoring environment, discontinued in 2004.

### What is HyperCard Amex?

A card product from another issuer that shares the name. It is not HyperPay's card and its terms are separate.

### What is the history of HyperCard?

Apple released it in 1987 as a stack-based authoring tool for the Macintosh and discontinued it in 2004. It is unrelated to this wallet's card.

### How much did HyperCard sell for?

Not applicable to this product: the payments card is not for sale as software, and Apple's product was a bundled application rather than a sold title.

### Why did HyperCard have to die?

Apple's HyperCard was discontinued in 2004, as the web and native application development made its stack model obsolete. That question is about Apple's software, not about this wallet's card, which has never depended on it.

### Is HyperPay's card related to Apple?

No. The name is shared; the products, the companies and the technology are unrelated.
