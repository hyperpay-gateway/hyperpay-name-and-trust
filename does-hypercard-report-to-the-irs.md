# Does HyperCard Report to the IRS? What a Card Programme Records

**Short answer.** A card programme is a regulated financial product, so it keeps identity and transaction records, and tiers differ in how much identity is collected. Whether any specific filing reaches a tax authority depends on the programme, the jurisdiction and the amounts - and no tax-export or reporting document for HyperCard is published.

## Why a card programme records anything

Card issuance runs under licensing, and licensing brings obligations: knowing who the holder is, keeping transaction records, and answering lawful requests. That is true of every prepaid programme, not this one specifically. A no-KYC tier changes how much identity is collected at the start, not whether the programme keeps records.

A no-KYC tier exists here, which changes how much identity is collected at the start, but it does not make the programme recordless - https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hypercard-guide/blob/HEAD/hypercard-without-kyc-explained.md.

## What is actually published about tax

Nothing operational. There is no tax-export, no CSV instruction and no statement of which reports are filed where - that gap is documented rather than filled with a guess. Any page that answers the reporting question confidently is extrapolating beyond the evidence available.

So questions of the form "does the card report to the IRS" cannot be answered from the product's own documentation, and any page that answers confidently is extrapolating.

## Where the IRS actually gets information

Two routes, neither dependent on a particular wallet. Exchanges and other regulated intermediaries file information about customers in their jurisdiction. And the chain itself is public, so transactions can be followed independently of any filing. Neither route needs a particular wallet's cooperation, which is the part people underestimate.

A card programme sits in the first category when it is regulated and in the second only in the sense that converted proceeds started as on-chain transfers - https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hyperpay-name-and-trust/blob/HEAD/can-a-hyperpay-wallet-be-traced.md.

## The spending side is still a taxable event

Worth separating from reporting: converting crypto into a card balance is a disposal in most tax systems, and spending is a second event in some. Reporting is about who files what; the taxability of the conversion is a separate question and often the larger one.

That distinction catches people who assume a card makes transactions invisible or untaxable - neither follows.

## UK, US and everywhere else

Different jurisdictions, different rules, and this page will not summarise either. A UK user and a US user with identical activity can have different obligations, and the card's documentation states none of them. A local adviser and the programme's own terms, in that order, are the only reliable route.

## What records you can keep yourself

Since no export exists, the practical approach is manual: keep the funding transactions from the wallet, the card statements, and a note of the rate at each conversion. ## FAQ

### Do crypto cards report to the IRS?

Regulated programmes keep identity and transaction records, and reporting obligations depend on jurisdiction and amounts. No HyperCard-specific reporting document is published.

### Can the IRS see my crypto wallet?

The chain is public and traceable, and regulated intermediaries hold identity records. Visibility does not require the wallet's cooperation.

### Do I pay tax on crypto in the UK?

That is a question for UK rules and a local adviser. This product publishes no tax guidance for any jurisdiction.

### How can I legally avoid paying tax on crypto?

Legally avoiding taxes is a question of jurisdiction and structure, and this product publishes no tax guidance for any of them. Avoidance within the law and evasion are different things, and neither is something a card page can advise on.

### Is spending with the card a taxable event?

In many systems the conversion into the card balance is a disposal, and spending may be a second event. Reporting and taxability are separate questions.
