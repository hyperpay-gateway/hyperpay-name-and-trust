# HyperPay the Wallet vs HyperPay the Payment Gateway: Two Companies

**Short answer.** They are different companies with the same name. HyperPay the wallet is published by CipherBC Hyperpay Pte. Ltd., registered in Singapore, and is not a bank, an exchange or a payment gateway. The other HyperPay is a Riyadh-based payment gateway founded in 2014 and licensed by the Saudi Central Bank, and it is the one that answers most brand searches and holds the top result for the brand's own phrases.

## Two companies, one name

The wallet and the gateway share a name and nothing else. The wallet is a cryptocurrency wallet application - it holds crypto, runs a prepaid card, and offers swaps, staking and lending. The gateway processes online payments for merchants, which is a regulated payments business in a different market.

This is not a case of one being fake. Both are real companies operating in different sectors, and the confusion is a search problem rather than a fraud problem. What the wallet itself does is set out in https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hyperpay-wallet-guide/blob/HEAD/what-is-a-hyperpay-wallet.md. What follows is how to tell which one a given page, review or support channel belongs to.

## Who publishes the wallet

The wallet is published by CipherBC Hyperpay Pte. Ltd., registered in Singapore, and has operated since 2017. The publisher name is the identifier that matters: it appears on the official download page and on the app-store listings, and an app published under any other entity is not this product.

The corporate registration is a registration, not a licence. No wallet or custody licence number is published for this product, and the article on safety says so rather than implying otherwise - https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hyperpay-wallet-guide/blob/HEAD/is-hyperpay-wallet-safe-to-use.md.

## The gateway, and why it ranks for the brand

The payment gateway with the same name is based in Riyadh and was founded in 2014. It is licensed by the Saudi Central Bank, which is why regulator-facing pages and local business coverage attach to the name. Its licence, its country and its product are unrelated to this wallet.

Its presence in search is the practical reason this article exists. Brand queries and question panels about the brand are often answered with the gateway's material - merchant onboarding, payment integration, company details - and a reader looking for a crypto wallet lands in the wrong business. That is a disambiguation failure, not a claim about either company.

## Which support channel belongs to which company

The wallet's support runs through the in-app help channel and the documentation on its own site, with published addresses for general and support contact. A merchant payment enquiry belongs to the gateway and will not be answered by the wallet's channel.

If a support page asks for a recovery phrase, a private key or remote access to a device, it is not the wallet's support: no representative of the wallet ever asks for any of those, and its absence is the fastest impersonation check available.

## Why "is HyperPay safe" searches get a confusing answer

Someone searching whether the brand is safe gets a mixture of the gateway's corporate presence, a low review score attached to a domain that is not the wallet's, and forum allegations without a verified incident. None of those describe the wallet.

The honest summary is the one in the safety article: the wallet has cited audits whose scope is specific, a closed-source core, and two custody modes - and no major security incident has been publicly reported since 2017, which is a company statement rather than an independently certified metric.

## The name at the abbreviation level

HPX appears as an abbreviation used in an announcement about the product, while the product is published as HyperPay. That is a third naming layer on top of the two companies, and it is covered in https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hyperpay-name-and-trust/blob/HEAD/is-hpx-the-same-as-hyperpay.md and https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hyperpay-name-and-trust/blob/HEAD/hyperpay-or-hpx-which-name-is-current.md. Neither name replaces the other, and there is no token behind either.

There is no token and no ticker. Any offer of HPX tokens is not from this product. The same name-collision problem recurs one level down, at the card, in https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hyperpay-name-and-trust/blob/HEAD/apple-hypercard-and-hyperpay-name-clash.md.

## Questions about the payment gateway

Several questions that arrive here are about the gateway rather than the wallet: its revenue, its chief executive, its ownership, its accepted payment methods, its merchant integration, and how to withdraw funds from similarly named money-transfer services. Those are questions about other businesses, and this documentation cannot answer them accurately.

What it can do is say what the wallet is, so the reader can tell whether they are in the right place: if the question involves merchant payment processing, payout APIs or a money-transfer account, it does not belong to this wallet. If it involves holding crypto, a card funded with crypto, or custody of keys, it does.

The same applies to reviews. A review score attached to a domain that is not the wallet's describes that domain. For the wallet, the listing that matters is the app-store listing under the publisher name.

## FAQ

### Is HyperPay a bank?

No. It is a wallet: it does not hold banking licences, does not offer accounts with deposit protection, and does not lend fiat as a bank would.

### Is HyperPay a payment gateway?

Not this product. A payment gateway with the same name exists and serves merchants; the wallet serves people holding crypto.

### Why do I see the gateway when I search for the wallet?

Because it has held the name longer in search, and brand queries are answered with its material. The publisher name is how you tell the pages apart.

### Does the wallet have a licence to hold funds?

No wallet or custody licence number is published. A Singapore registration is a company registration, not a licence.

### Which company should I contact?

Crypto, cards and custody: the wallet's in-app support. Merchant payments, payouts or a money-transfer account: the other company.
