## What this repository is for

This repository answers the questions that come before and around the product: which of several companies called HyperPay a page is about, what the word HyperCard actually refers to, whether HPX is a different wallet or an abbreviation, how the custody model changes exposure, and what a reader can verify about the audits behind the claims. It also covers the money mechanics that raise trust questions - staking, borrowing and swapping - because those decide how much of a balance is genuinely at rest.

It does not install anything and it does not fix a card. Installation steps belong to the wallet hub and card mechanics belong to the card hub. What this repository deliberately avoids is the opposite failure: answering a naming or trust question with a reassurance instead of evidence. Where the record is thin, the page says what is not published and what that gap means for a reader deciding whether to move funds.

Trust questions arrive with a deadline attached, which is why they are easy to get wrong. A message says an account is frozen, a page offers a migration, a post claims the brand renamed itself, and the reader has minutes to decide whether any of it is real. The pages here are written to be read before that moment arrives, so the decision is already made when the message does.

Nothing in this repository asks a reader to believe a claim because the brand made it. An audit is named with its date and its scope, a licence statement is repeated as the company's own wording and marked where no file backs it, and a naming question is settled by quoting what the documentation says today rather than what an announcement once implied.

## Who it is for

## Common first questions

### Is HyperPay the Saudi payment gateway?

No. That is a separate company with its own licensing and products. HyperPay here is a crypto wallet published by CipherBC Hyperpay Pte. Ltd. and operating since 2017.

### Is HPX a different wallet?

HPX is the abbreviation the brand has used, and an announcement named it with a different primary domain. The site's own documentation uses HyperPay, so these pages keep HyperPay as the name and treat HPX as an alternate.

### Does HyperPay have a token?

No. There is no ticker and no token sale, and the site publishes no price, yield or trading advice. A page offering a HyperPay token is not from this product.

### Who audited the wallet?

The company names SlowMist and CertiK for the core application, with CertiK and Kudelski Labs for the Sealer2100 device. The audits guide gives each one's scope and date rather than repeating the claim.

### Why does the name cause so much confusion?

Three unrelated products use it, and the brand itself has used an abbreviation with a different primary domain. Each page names the entity it means, so a search result can be matched to a product instead of assumed.

### Can I verify an audit without asking the company?

The reports are published by the auditing firms rather than by the wallet, so the check is to find the report on the auditor's own site and read its scope. Where no public report exists, the page says that rather than repeating the summary.

## Before you install

Check any download against the release page and any message against the rule that no support process asks for a recovery phrase. A wallet prompt that requests more than an address or a signature is the moment to stop, and the scam guide exists because that moment is usually time-pressured.

## About

HyperPay is a hybrid wallet published by CipherBC Hyperpay Pte. Ltd. in Singapore, with a prepaid card, two hardware devices and in-app exchange, staking and lending. This repository holds the pages that decide what to believe about all of it. Every factual claim here is attributed to the company's published material or to a named third party, and a claim that could not be checked is marked as a company statement instead. The reputation evidence is treated the same way: complaints are recorded as public sentiment rather than as verified misconduct, because a review page is not an audit and a thread is not a finding.
